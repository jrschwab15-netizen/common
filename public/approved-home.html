PUBLIC PROBLEMS. SHARED VISIBILITY.

Public problems belong in public view.

A broken sidewalk affects everyone who uses it. An overflowing bin is more than one person's complaint. Reporting it should help the whole community see what needs attention—and what happens next.

A service request

Ask for something to be done.

  1. 01 Send an issue to the responsible service.
  2. 02 Receive a request number or status.
  3. 03 Follow the agency's response.
  4. 04 A closed request may still need a closer look.
A shared public record

See it. Follow it. Verify it.

  1. 01 Find reviewed issues on a community map.
  2. 02 See photos and time since first reported.
  3. 03 Follow the case history in one place.
  4. 04 Distinguish agency closure from a verified fix.

311 and municipal reporting services help route requests; some also publish maps and status updates. Common complements those services with a community-facing record built around reviewed evidence, issue history, and independently verified outcomes. Common is not an official municipal service and does not guarantee agency delivery.

MORE THAN THE STREET OUTSIDE

Our common places. Our shared concern.

Streets, parks, playgrounds, airports, public terminals, train and bus stations, public buildings, and waterfronts all deserve attention when they are managed by a government agency.

Maintenance and access

Litter, overflowing bins, damaged paths, broken lighting, blocked access, and other public-space conditions.

Non-emergency hazards

Report insect nests or other conditions affecting safe public access from a safe distance. Never disturb a nest or enter a restricted area for a photo.

The right responsible agency

Our goal is to send eligible reports to the town, municipality, or authority responsible for that place. Responsibility and delivery must be verified; automatic agency delivery is not yet enabled.

Designed for a moment, not a form

Three steps. That's the whole report.

Walking by a problem? Tap Quick Report, continue as a guest or sign in, confirm the location, take a photo, and add a note only if you want to. After reporting, guests can choose to save their report to an account. Common takes care of the behind-the-scenes checks before anything appears publicly.

Emergency hazards require immediate emergency or utility reporting instead.

How it works

Small actions. Real public visibility.

01

Spot something.

Trash, potholes, graffiti, or public infrastructure that needs attention. Report it with a photo and confirmed location.

02

Make it visible.

Common checks the location and privacy safeguards, then publishes eligible problems and their report history.

03

See what changed.

Track agency updates, time since first reported, and independent community evidence that a problem was fixed.

For every community

A clearer picture of public maintenance.

Every town should have an accessible record of reported issues, agency activity, and verified repairs. More informed residents. Better conversations. Evidence everyone can see.

We measure what is reported and confirmed—not a town's character or the intentions of its officials. Reports without adequate geographic coverage are labeled accordingly.
